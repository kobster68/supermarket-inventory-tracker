\subsection{Viewpoint Definitions}
\label{sec:viewpoints}

This draft's key view is the Components-and-Connectors view, and the C\&C viewpoint is
defined in full below in the form the SEI Views and Beyond template uses. The summary of
which stakeholders each viewpoint serves is presented with the stakeholder matrix in
Section~\ref{sec:stakeholders}. The module decomposition viewpoint used by the primary
view (Section~\ref{sec:module-view}) follows the SEI template's module decomposition style
(``Software Architecture Document Template,'' \S\,Module Decomposition Viewpoint; Bass,
Clements, and Kazman 10--11) and is not restated here. The Allocation viewpoint
below defines the deployment view delivered in Section~\ref{sec:allocation-view}.

\subsubsection{Components-and-Connectors Viewpoint Definition}

\paragraph{Abstract.}
A Components-and-Connectors view shows the system at runtime: the units that compute
and hold state (components) and the pathways through which they interact (connectors).
It is the view that answers how an \InventoryEvent travels from a store to the \DC and
on to the clients that need it, and what happens when the link between them fails
(Bass, Clements, and Kazman 335).

\paragraph{Stakeholders and their concerns addressed.}
Store managers and store operators who need the local inventory to stay reachable
during a WAN outage; \DC managers who rely on the master record and the shipping
orders; operators who need the acknowledgement and replay rules to be explicit;
security reviewers who need the store-to-\DC channels marked; and developers of new
subscribing clients. Their concerns are the availability, delivery, performance,
security and modifiability scenarios of Section~\ref{sec:driving-requirements}.

\paragraph{Elements, relations, properties, and constraints.}
The elements are the runtime components \POSAdapter, \StoreAgent, \StoreLocalInventory,
\Outbox, \CFE, \DCAgent, \CentralMasterInventory, \ShippingOrderFulfillment,
\StoreTrackingClient and \ReportingClient, together with the external POS system and the
external \AnalyticsClient at the boundary and the store and \DC managers as actors. The
relations are five connector types:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{<<pub-sub>>} --- the single n-ary \IEP connector, with the \CFE as the
  pub-sub event bus between the publishers and the subscribers;
  \item \texttt{<<event stream>>} --- a one-way stream of events with no reply, such as
  the external POS system's sales records into the \POSAdapter;
  \item \texttt{<<request/reply>>} --- synchronous calls, such as a manager or a client
  querying the \CentralMasterInventory;
  \item \texttt{<<data access>>} --- a component reading or writing its own persistent
  store;
  \item \texttt{<<local call>>} --- an in-process hand-off between a component and its
  own boundary element.
\end{itemize}
The properties recorded for each element and connector are location, persistence,
protocol, security, delivery semantics and throughput.
The constraints are listed with the view in Section~\ref{sec:cc-view}.

\paragraph{Language(s) to model or represent conforming views.}
A conforming view is drawn as a UML component diagram for its primary presentation and
as UML sequence diagrams for its behavior, authored in PlantUML with the shared
notation key of Section~\ref{sec:views} (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 341; Hawker,
``Architecture Documentation,'' slides 14--17).

\paragraph{Applicable evaluation and consistency criteria.}
A conforming view is complete and consistent when every element and connector in a
diagram has an entry in the element catalog and the reverse, and when every scenario
that touches the runtime has a path through the view.

\paragraph{Viewpoint source.}
The fourth edition of \emph{Software Architecture in Practice}, Chapter 22, and the SEI
Views and Beyond Software Architecture Document template (Bass, Clements, and Kazman
333--338; ``Software Architecture Document Template,'' \S\,Viewpoint Definitions).

\subsubsection{Allocation Viewpoint Definition}
\paragraph{Abstract.}
The deployment viewpoint maps runtime software and persistent state to execution
hosts and storage, with the network links connecting those hosts. It answers
where each component runs and what remains available when a process, host or
link fails (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 15).

\paragraph{Stakeholders and concerns.}
Store and \DC operators need placements, capacity obligations and recovery
sources; store managers need local access during WAN outages; security reviewers
need system boundaries and protected network paths.

\paragraph{Elements, relations and properties.}
Elements are execution/storage nodes, network links, runtime components and
persistent stores. Nesting means allocated-to; links mean node-to-node
communication or local storage access. Properties include multiplicity, workload,
protocol protection, persistence and failure/recovery assumptions. Each internal
runtime component has one baseline placement; external systems remain outside
our boundary, while the store-to-\DC WAN remains inside it.

\paragraph{Notation and evaluation.}
We use a PlantUML deployment diagram with a key and a node catalog. Every C\&C
component/store must appear once in the baseline component-to-node mapping;
placements must agree with the diagram. Capacity checks include live and replay
traffic. Failure cases identify surviving state and the authoritative recovery
log without assuming redundant hardware that the deployment does not show.

\paragraph{Viewpoint source.}
The deployment structure in \emph{Software Architecture in Practice}, fourth
edition (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 15), organized as a SEI view packet.
