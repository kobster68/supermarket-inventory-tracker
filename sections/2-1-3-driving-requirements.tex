\subsubsection{Significant Driving Requirements}
\label{sec:driving-requirements}

\paragraph{Functional requirements.}
The brief asks the system to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=FR-\arabic*,nosep,leftmargin=3.2em]
  \item record sales in real time and keep a local inventory in each store, plus a
  master inventory that covers all store and \DC stock;
  \item make shipping orders from minimum stock quantities and current sales;
  \item provide a front-end and an exchange protocol that send alerts to the central
  inventory, to store tracking, and to a variable number of other clients;
  \item support product families nested to any depth;
  \item give managers lowest-stock, overstocked-item and product-family total-value
  reports;
  \item connect to external subsystems with the exchanged information defined.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Quality-attribute requirements.}
The quality-attribute requirements that drive the architecture are recorded as
six-part scenarios (Hawker, ``Quality Attributes, Quality Scenarios, and Design
Tactics,'' slides 8--9). Each scenario has a frozen identifier; the full set of
identifiers and their one-line titles is listed below. The brief states
availability, performance, scalability, security and usability. We found the hidden
requirements for modifiability, integrability, testability and portability in its
variable clients, nested families and external systems. Where the brief gives no
number, the response measure is our own target, which we will confirm with the
stakeholders.

\qascenario{AV-1}
  {The wide-area network between a store and the \DC (external physical infrastructure).}
  {An omission fault: the store loses its network link to the \DC.}
  {The communication channel between the \StoreAgent and the \CFE.}
  {Normal operation.}
  {The \StoreAgent keeps serving reads and updates against the \StoreLocalInventory, logs the fault, and appends each outbound \InventoryEvent to the durable \Outbox for delivery when the link returns.}
  {The \StoreLocalInventory stays readable and updatable at least 99\% of the time; this single outage lasts no more than 30 minutes; no buffered event is lost on reconnect.}

\qascenario{AV-2}
  {The WAN or the \CFE (transient fault).}
  {An \InventoryEvent or its \Acknowledgement is lost between the \Outbox and the \CFE.}
  {The \Outbox, the \IEP connector and the \CFE.}
  {Normal operation, 3 to 10 stores at full rate.}
  {The \Outbox keeps the event and sends it again with its original ID until the \CFE acknowledges it. The \CFE and the \CentralMasterInventory discard duplicates by event ID and sequence.}
  {100\% of store events reach the \DC; 0 events lost; 0 events applied twice.}

\qascenario{AV-3}
  {An internal software fault at the \DC.}
  {The \CentralMasterInventory process crashes.}
  {The \CentralMasterInventory and its database.}
  {Normal operation; stores keep publishing.}
  {The \CFE keeps accepting and storing events. The process restarts, runs \opSubscribe, and runs \opReplay from its last applied sequence.}
  {0 events lost; the master record is current again within 30 minutes; store operation stops for 0 seconds.}

\qascenario{PERF-1}
  {The POS system of one store.}
  {Sales and stock events arrive at 100 events/s for a sustained period.}
  {The \POSAdapter, the \StoreAgent, the \StoreLocalInventory and the \Outbox.}
  {Normal operation, peak trading.}
  {The \StoreAgent applies each event locally and appends it to the \Outbox.}
  {100 events/s sustained; each event is in the \StoreLocalInventory within 1 s; the \Outbox backlog does not grow.}

\qascenario{PERF-2}
  {The \StoreAgent{}s of ten stores.}
  {All stores publish at full rate: 1{,}000 events/s at the \DC.}
  {The \CFE, its event store and its subscribers.}
  {Maximum scale: ten stores and one \DC.}
  {The \CFE persists each event, acknowledges it, and publishes it to every subscriber.}
  {1{,}000 events/s sustained; each event acknowledged within 1 s; no queue at the \DC grows.}

\qascenario{PERF-3}
  {A \DC manager.}
  {Asks for the total value of a product family with nested sub-families.}
  {The \ReportingClient and the \CentralMasterInventory.}
  {1{,}000 events/s arrive at the \DC.}
  {The \ReportingClient goes through the family tree and returns the report.}
  {The report returns within 10 s; the \CFE still sustains 1{,}000 events/s.}

\qascenario{SEC-1}
  {An unauthenticated person or process, inside or outside the network.}
  {Tries to read or change a store or master inventory database.}
  {The \StoreLocalInventory, the master inventory database and their access paths.}
  {Normal operation.}
  {The system rejects all access without the credentials of an authorized manager or agent, and logs the attempt.}
  {100\% of attempts rejected and logged; 0 records read or changed.}

\qascenario{SEC-2}
  {An attacker on the WAN.}
  {Captures or changes \IEP messages between a store and the \DC.}
  {The channel between the \Outbox and the \CFE.}
  {Normal operation.}
  {TLS encrypts each message and checks its integrity. The \CFE rejects a changed message and does not acknowledge it, so the \Outbox sends it again.}
  {0 events readable by the attacker; 100\% of changed messages rejected; 0 changed events applied.}

\qascenario{SEC-3}
  {An authenticated store manager.}
  {Tries to read or change the inventory of a different store.}
  {The access control of the \StoreAgent and the \CentralMasterInventory.}
  {Normal operation.}
  {The system denies the request and logs it.}
  {100\% of out-of-scope requests denied and logged; 0 records changed.}

\qascenario{SCAL-1}
  {Chain management.}
  {The chain adds stores, from three to ten.}
  {The \StoreAgent deployment and the \CFE.}
  {Normal operation; stores are added one at a time.}
  {We deploy one configured \StoreAgent for each new store. It publishes to the \CFE like the existing stores.}
  {0 code changes to the \CFE or the subscribers; \scnref{PERF-2} still holds at ten stores.}

\qascenario{SCAL-2}
  {A client developer.}
  {A new alerting client subscribes to the \CFE at runtime.}
  {The \CFE.}
  {Normal operation at full load.}
  {The \CFE registers the subscription and sends the client each new event on its topics.}
  {0 \CFE restarts; 0 events lost to the existing subscribers; the client receives the next published event.}

\qascenario{USE-1}
  {A store manager who is new to the system.}
  {Wants to produce a shipping order.}
  {\ShippingOrderGen and its manager screen.}
  {Normal operation.}
  {The system proposes an order from minimum stock quantities and sales. The manager checks it and confirms it.}
  {After at most 1 hour of training, 9 of 10 managers complete an order without help in less than 5 minutes.}

\qascenario{USE-2}
  {A \DC manager who is new to the system.}
  {Reads the lowest-stock, overstocked-item and family-value reports.}
  {The \ReportingClient.}
  {Normal operation.}
  {Each report shows its type, scope and units with the data.}
  {After at most 1 hour of training, managers answer 9 of 10 questions about the reports correctly.}

\qascenario{MOD-1}
  {A developer.}
  {Adds a new client type that receives \InventoryEvent alerts.}
  {The new client, the \IEP and the \CFE.}
  {Design time.}
  {The developer writes the client against the \IEP and subscribes it to the \CFE.}
  {0 changed modules outside the new client; done in at most 5 person-days.}

\qascenario{MOD-2}
  {A \DC manager or a developer.}
  {The product-family hierarchy gains one more nesting level.}
  {The product-family model and the \ReportingClient.}
  {Runtime, as a data change.}
  {The recursive family model accepts the new level as data; the reports include it.}
  {0 code changes; 100\% of family-value reports correct for the new level.}

\qascenario{INT-1}
  {A supplier integrator.}
  {Connects an external system, for example a new supplier/ERP system, over the \IEP.}
  {The \SupplierGateway and the \IEP.}
  {Development time.}
  {The integrator writes an adapter that maps the external format to \IEP messages.}
  {Changes stay in one boundary element; done in at most 10 person-days.}

\qascenario{INT-2}
  {Client developers.}
  {Add a variable number of alerting clients through the \CFE.}
  {The \CFE and its fan-out.}
  {Normal operation at full load.}
  {Each client subscribes over the \IEP; the \CFE sends events to all of them.}
  {With up to 10 subscribers, \scnref{PERF-2} still holds; 0 \CFE code changes.}

\qascenario{TEST-1}
  {A tester.}
  {Drops 10\% of the \IEP messages and acknowledgements during a load test.}
  {The \Outbox, the \CFE and the \CentralMasterInventory.}
  {Test environment, 100 events/s per store.}
  {The test harness controls the loss and compares the sent events with the applied events by ID and sequence.}
  {The test finds 100\% of lost or duplicate events; one test run takes at most 1 hour.}

\qascenario{TEST-2}
  {A tester.}
  {Stops a \StoreAgent or cuts the WAN to measure detection and failover.}
  {The \StoreAgent and its supervision.}
  {Test environment.}
  {The harness records the time of the failure, the detection and the restored access.}
  {Recovery time measured to within 1 s; the test shows an outage of at most 30 minutes.}

\qascenario{PORT-1}
  {An operator or a developer.}
  {Moves a store or \DC node to a different operating system or hardware.}
  {The \StoreAgent or the \DC services.}
  {Maintenance window.}
  {We rebuild the node with a platform adapter and new configuration; the \IEP does not change.}
  {0 changes to the \IEP or other nodes; done in at most 5 person-days; all \texttt{AV} and \texttt{PERF} scenarios still pass.}
\endgroup
