\subsubsection{Analysis Results}
\label{sec:analysis-results}

These workload calculations size the selected design and define test inputs; they
are not measured performance results.

\paragraph{Local availability.}
For an observation period \(T\), 99\% availability permits \(0.01T\) downtime
(Bass et al. 53): an illustrative 30 days permits 432 minutes.
The observation period and accounting rules require agreement. The separate maximum
individual local-inventory outage remains 30 minutes; a total budget does not override it.

\paragraph{Store buffer capacity.}
At 100 inventory events per second, a 30-minute WAN interruption produces
\(B=100\times1{,}800=180{,}000\) buffered inventory events per store.
For persisted event size \(s\), reserve at least \(Bs\) bytes, plus existing queued
work, confirmed orders, storage overhead and headroom. Separate local recovery
history also needs space. This WAN duration is a sizing assumption, not a network
repair guarantee or a local-access interruption.

\paragraph{Post-outage catch-up (\scnref{PERF-4}).}
For full catch-up including continuing arrivals \(\lambda=100\), clearing \(B\)
within \(D=600\) seconds needs effective service rate
\(\mu\geq\lambda+B/D=400\) events per second. Equal arrival and service rates never
drain the queue. With nine other stores at normal rate, one store recovering at this
minimum needs 1{,}300 store events per second at the \DC.
The selected capacity target is 2{,}000 events per second per recovering store
(Sections~\ref{sec:cc-view}, \ref{sec:allocation-view}). Counting this as that store's
combined live/replay inventory rate, \(k\) simultaneous recovering stores require
\((10-k)100+k(2{,}000)\): 2{,}900 for one, or 20{,}000 for ten, before \DC events,
orders, retries and fan-out. If 2{,}000 denotes replay traffic alone, add another
100 events per second for each recovering store. The full-catch-up model is a sizing assumption; the delivery milestone
ending \scnref{PERF-4}'s ten minutes still needs explicit agreement.

\paragraph{Central work and retention.}
Three stores produce 300 new inventory events per second; ten produce 1{,}000.
Ten consumers following all sources can require 10{,}000 deliveries per second at
that live rate, plus acknowledgements. Local filtering does not remove this fan-out
cost. The \CFE stores one accepted message with subscription progress, rather than
requiring a separate log copy per subscriber. Even with prompt processing
acknowledgements, its 60-minute post-acknowledgement window represents
\(1{,}000\times3{,}600=3{,}600{,}000\) live inventory records. Budget their persisted
sizes, identities, indexes and checkpoints, plus unacknowledged history and recovery
bursts. The window starts after the last required cumulative acknowledgement;
disconnected active subscriptions pin unread history, so 60 minutes is not a maximum
retention duration (Section~\ref{sec:iep}). Capacity exhaustion requires backpressure.

\paragraph{Validation and timing alignment.}
Acceptance, first delivery and completed consumer processing are distinct milestones.
\scnref{PERF-2} and the \IEP must agree on clock boundaries and per-event versus
percentile limits before those timings become acceptance criteria. The calculations
above establish neither latency nor successful end-to-end recovery. Load and fault
tests must combine live traffic, replay, report queries and consumer acknowledgements,
and verify no committed event is lost or applied twice.
