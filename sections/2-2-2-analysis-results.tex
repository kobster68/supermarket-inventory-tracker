\subsubsection{Analysis Results}
\label{sec:analysis-results}

These workload calculations size the selected design and define test inputs; they
are not measured performance results.

\paragraph{Local availability.}
For an observation period \(T\), 99\% availability permits \(0.01T\) downtime
(Bass et al. 53): an illustrative 30 days permits 432 minutes.
The observation period and accounting rules require agreement. The separate maximum
individual local-inventory outage remains 30 minutes; a total budget does not override it.

\paragraph{Store buffer capacity.}
Size buffering in days: at 100 inventory events per second, \(d\) days of WAN outage
produce \(B=100\times86{,}400d=8{,}640{,}000d\) inventory events per store.
The 30-minute \scnref{PERF-4} test produces \(B=180{,}000\) events.
For persisted event size \(s\), reserve at least \(Bs\) bytes, plus existing queued
work, confirmed orders, storage overhead and headroom. Separate local recovery
history also needs space. Choose \(d\) as a provisioning allowance, not a maximum
outage duration. Finite capacity can still fill, at which point the \IEP rejects local
updates atomically. A snapshot restores surviving current state after reconnect but
does not recover lost event-by-event history or extend storage capacity.

\paragraph{Post-outage catch-up (\scnref{PERF-4}).}
\scnref{PERF-4} measures from link restoration until every outage-buffered event has
been processed and acknowledged by every required consumer, within ten minutes.
For the stronger sizing model of full catch-up including continuing arrivals
\(\lambda=100\), clearing the 30-minute backlog \(B\)
within \(D=600\) seconds needs effective service rate
\(\mu\geq\lambda+B/D=400\) events per second. Equal arrival and service rates never
drain the queue. With nine other stores at normal rate, one store recovering at this
minimum needs 1{,}300 store events per second at the \DC.
The selected capacity target is 2{,}000 events per second per recovering store
(Sections~\ref{sec:cc-view}, \ref{sec:allocation-view}), including that store's live
traffic and replay. Thus \(k\) simultaneous recovering stores require
\((10-k)100+k(2{,}000)\): 2{,}900 for one, or 20{,}000 for ten, before \DC events,
orders, retries and fan-out.
For sustained effective processing rate \(\mu>\lambda\), ideal full catch-up time is
\(D=B/(\mu-\lambda)\). At the selected 2{,}000/s target, one day's backlog takes about
76 minutes in this model. Longer outages increase recovery time. These estimates
assume the full pipeline sustains the rate and exclude startup delays; timed tests
must establish completion through the slowest required consumer.

\paragraph{Central work and retention.}
Three stores produce 300 new inventory events per second; ten produce 1{,}000.
Ten consumers following all sources can require 10{,}000 deliveries per second at
that live rate, plus acknowledgements. Local filtering does not remove this fan-out
cost. The \CFE stores one accepted message with subscription progress, rather than
requiring a separate log copy per subscriber. A message is eligible for pruning once
every relevant active subscription has acknowledged durable processing. Retained
volume therefore depends on subscriber lag and recovery bursts. Budget unread messages,
identity records, indexes, checkpoints and headroom. Disconnected active subscriptions
pin unread history (Section~\ref{sec:iep}). Capacity exhaustion requires backpressure.
Analytics adds read-query work rather than subscriber deliveries.

\paragraph{Standby capacity.}
The \StandbyMaster adds one delivery and durable update per event, plus acknowledgement traffic.
At ten stores' live rate, that is 1{,}000 additional inventory-event deliveries per
second compared with one master, before \DC events, orders and recovery traffic.
Include this subscription in the consumer count above. Provision its host for event
processing, queries after failover and copying state to a recovering master.
Measure standby lag under this combined load because queued work affects how current
its answers are when it takes over.

\paragraph{Validation and timing alignment.}
Acceptance, first delivery and completed consumer processing are distinct milestones.
\scnref{PERF-2} starts both clocks at the store's first transmission: publisher
acceptance must return within one second, and all required consumers must process and
acknowledge within five seconds, both at the 95th percentile. The \IEP's separate
two-second first-delivery target excludes processing and does not demonstrate the
five-second requirement. The calculations above establish neither latency nor successful
end-to-end recovery. Load and fault
tests must combine live traffic, replay, report and analytics queries, and consumer acknowledgements,
and verify no committed event is lost or applied twice.
