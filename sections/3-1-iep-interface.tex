\paragraph{\IEP interface specification.}
\label{sec:iep}
\subparagraph{Identity, purpose, and provided/required sides.}
We define version 1.0 of the \IEP to connect store and \DC publishers
to runtime subscribers through the \CFE.
The provided side comprises the six operations below; the required side comprises
durable storage, a subscriber registry, secure channels, a product catalog and clocks
(Bass, Clements, and Kazman 230). Publishers retain unacknowledged events and
subscribers process them idempotently.

\subparagraph{Messages and source streams.}
We use case-sensitive fields and nonempty identifiers compared exactly;
the wire encoding preserves their types.

\begin{description}[nosep,leftmargin=1em,style=nextline]
\item[\InventoryEvent]
\texttt{eventId}: globally unique identifier, unchanged on retry;
\texttt{source}: \texttt{store/<storeId>} or \texttt{dc/<dcId>};
\texttt{product}: stable product identifier;
\texttt{quantityDelta}: nonzero signed 64-bit stock-unit delta (positive adds);
\texttt{timestamp}: UTC \texttt{YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss.sssZ};
\texttt{sequence}: persistent positive 64-bit counter, consecutive per source from 1.
Retries preserve all fields; sequence, not timestamp, determines order.

\item[\ShippingOrder]
Uses the same \texttt{eventId}, \texttt{source}, \texttt{timestamp} and
\texttt{sequence} fields, plus \texttt{orderId}, \texttt{destination} (a \DC
identifier) and nonempty \texttt{lines} of product ID and positive 64-bit quantity.
The \StoreAgent raises an order when stock reaches its minimum; after manager
confirmation it enters the \Outbox for \ShippingOrderFulfillment at the \DC.
Only a store may publish an order for itself. Both message types share one
sequence per source and the same ordering, retry and acknowledgement rules.

\item[\Acknowledgement]
\texttt{kind=accepted}: the \CFE returns \texttt{eventId}, \texttt{source},
\texttt{sequence} and \texttt{acceptedThrough}, its highest contiguous persisted
source sequence. \texttt{kind=applied}: a subscriber sends \texttt{subscriptionId},
\texttt{source} and \texttt{appliedThrough}, meaning all messages through that
sequence are durably processed or intentionally ignored by its local filter.
This cumulative cursor is distinct from publisher acceptance.

\item[Subscription]
\texttt{subscriberId}: stable authenticated client ID;
\texttt{subscriptionId}: identifier assigned by the \CFE;
\texttt{sourceFilter}: an authorized exact source or \texttt{*} for all authorized
sources. The \CFE retains a durable per-subscription/source acknowledged delivery
position, \texttt{appliedThrough}. Recovery positions are per subscription and source.
\end{description}

We use one stream per source, with no topics or family routing. Each subscription
receives every message in its selected source streams and filters locally.
The \AnalyticsClient has read-only scope: it may subscribe, replay and resync its
authorized streams and acknowledge its own progress, but cannot publish either
message type or change stock/orders.

\subparagraph{Resources and operations.}
We specify each operation through its syntax, effects and errors
(Bass, Clements, and Kazman 219).
All requests require authenticated, authorized version-1.0 sessions; \texttt{?} means optional.
Common errors: \texttt{INVALID} (fields/version), \texttt{UNAUTHORIZED} (identity/scope),
\texttt{UNAVAILABLE}, \texttt{BUSY(retryAfterMs)} (positive millisecond delay),
\texttt{TIMEOUT} (uncertain outcome).

\begin{description}[nosep,leftmargin=1em,style=nextline]
\item[\opPublish\texttt{(message)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Submit an authorized source's next \InventoryEvent or \ShippingOrder.
\emph{Post:} Persist message and identity/sequence, then \opAck and fan-out;
identical retries create no extra entry.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{OUT\_OF\_ORDER} (returns expected sequence) for gaps,
\texttt{CONFLICT} for reused IDs/sequences with changed content,
\texttt{UNKNOWN\_PRODUCT}. Rejection never advances the source position.
\item[\opAck\texttt{(acknowledgement)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} \texttt{accepted} confirms \CFE persistence;
\texttt{applied} reports an owned active subscription's durable processing progress.
\emph{Post:} For \texttt{accepted}, the publisher (the store's \Outbox, or
\DCAgent) saves the maximum of its position and \texttt{acceptedThrough} before
pruning. For \texttt{applied}, the \CFE durably saves the maximum of its stored
cursor and \texttt{appliedThrough}; it must not exceed the contiguous position
sent to that subscription. Repeated acknowledgements never move either cursor backwards.
\emph{Errors:} Reject malformed/mismatched or unauthenticated confirmations as
\texttt{INVALID} or \texttt{UNAUTHORIZED} without advancing state;
\texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION} or \texttt{INVALID\_CURSOR} for invalid subscriber progress.
Lost confirmations cause unchanged retries.
\item[\opSubscribe\texttt{(subscriberId, sourceFilter)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Provisioned client opens a receive channel for authorized sources.
\emph{Post:} Persist/return subscription and \texttt{startAfter}, the per-source
accepted positions (zero if empty). Live events follow these positions; history
requires \opReplay. Identical active requests return the same record/positions.
The subscription's initial \texttt{appliedThrough} is \texttt{startAfter}; older
history is available only while retained. Disconnection does not remove a subscription.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIBER}; rejection creates nothing.
\item[\opUnsubscribe\texttt{(subscriberId, subscriptionId)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Owner removes its subscription.
\emph{Post:} Persist removal; stop scheduling deliveries, allowing in-flight messages.
Owner retries succeed while removal records exist; logs remain unchanged.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION} without active/removal record.
\item[\opReplay\texttt{(role, source, afterSequence, subscriptionId?, events?)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Nonnegative exclusive cursor. For \texttt{publisher}, send
ordered retained events after the publisher's saved position; for \texttt{subscriber},
identify an owned active subscription, omit events and use its durable cursor.
\emph{Post:} Publisher events follow \opPublish individually; partial failure
identifies the first failed event. \Subscriber replay returns all retained messages in
source order through a captured high-water mark, then \texttt{scannedThrough}
(that mark); live delivery follows without gaps. Subscribers acknowledge through
that mark only after processing or locally ignoring every returned message.
Retries may duplicate delivery.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors and those of \opPublish; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION},
\texttt{INVALID\_CURSOR} (subscriber ahead), \texttt{HISTORY\_EXPIRED}.
Publisher-ahead positions require \opResync rather than skipping.
\item[\opResync\texttt{(role, source, lastKnownSequence, subscriptionId?)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Authorized publisher/subscriber reconciles a nonnegative
position; subscribers identify an owned active subscription.
\emph{Post:} Return \texttt{source}, the supplied \texttt{lastKnownSequence},
\texttt{acceptedThrough}, \texttt{earliestRetained} (first replayable sequence;
accepted position plus one for an empty log), and, for subscribers, the \CFE's
stored \texttt{appliedThrough}. These are durable watermarks, not receipt-in-memory
positions. A publisher compares its saved acknowledgement with \texttt{acceptedThrough}
and resends retained messages after the latter. A subscriber compares its local
durable cursor with the returned positions and requests \opReplay after its local
cursor; a higher server-side acknowledgement never proves restored local state.
If that cursor precedes retained history, restore a consistent snapshot/backup
before replay. Store-local inventory rebuilds from its own durable log/snapshot,
using local applied/appended positions, before reconciling publisher delivery.
No stock or sequence is reset by this operation.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION},
\texttt{INVALID\_CURSOR} (subscriber ahead), \texttt{RECOVERY\_REQUIRED} with
bounds for missing history. Restore a consistent snapshot/backup and positions
before replay; reconciliation cannot reconstruct missing events.
\end{description}

\subparagraph{Shared semantics and restrictions.}
We restore writable logs, identity records, source positions and the registry before
serving requests. Each source has one logical publisher: its \StoreAgent
through the \Outbox, or the \DCAgent; failover prevents concurrent sequence allocation.
Every request and delivery is authorized. Local inventory, sequence and \Outbox
append commit atomically. We preserve order per source, not globally; subscribers
atomically save changes, deduplication and processing cursors. Identity records
survive restarts throughout retry/replay history; unverifiable old events require recovery.

Each publisher owns its acknowledged position (the \Outbox for a store, the \DCAgent for the \DC), saved before
pruning its outbound copy only after \CFE acceptance. This does not prune the
separate local log/snapshot needed for store recovery. Clients subscribe before receiving and recover using separate subscriber
cursors. Transient errors are retried unchanged with capped exponential backoff/jitter,
respecting \texttt{retryAfterMs}; unacknowledged events are never discarded.
Invalid requests require correction; gaps use \opReplay or \opResync
(Bass, Clements, and Kazman 228). We bound batches and queues, returning
\texttt{BUSY} before admission, and isolate slow subscribers for log-based catch-up.
Offline updates continue until \Outbox exhaustion, which rejects updates atomically.
Expired history never silently advances cursors.

\subparagraph{Retention and pruning.}
The \CFE may prune a message only after every active subscription for its source
has acknowledged through its sequence and a further 60 minutes has elapsed.
The timer starts when the last required acknowledgement arrives; without a relevant
subscription it starts at durable acceptance. A new subscription covers messages
after its \texttt{startAfter}; earlier history is best-effort replay while retained.
A disconnected subscription remains active and pins unread history; only explicit
\opUnsubscribe removes its obligation. Pruning reclaims eligible records to control
storage growth; a stalled subscription can still exhaust capacity, which triggers
backpressure rather than silent deletion. Identity records remain available at least
as long as the corresponding replay history and supported retry window.

\subparagraph{Variability, limits, and quality properties.}
We design for the required 100 events/s/store for ten stores (1{,}000/s), with additional
capacity for \DC traffic, retries and fan-out. Clients use \opSubscribe and
\opUnsubscribe at runtime without restarts or publisher changes. Each deployment
declares tested client/queue limits and returns \texttt{BUSY} at capacity.
The decided catch-up capacity is 2{,}000 events/s per recovering store while live
sales continue (\scnref{PERF-4}); this is a design requirement, not a measured result.
We use 1--100-message replay batches and the retention rule above, provisioning
capacity for pinned history and recovery traffic. Our proposed 95th-percentile targets are
1 second to \opAck and 2 seconds to first delivery, measured from transmission
at live load with healthy infrastructure, no backlog and within client limits;
application processing is excluded. We provide eventual, at-least-once delivery
assuming connectivity returns, retries continue and capacity/storage remain available.
If required history was already pruned, recovery needs consistent snapshots/backups; permanent loss of
all copies is unrecoverable. We leave system sizing to Section~\ref{sec:analysis-results}.

\subparagraph{Element requirements.}
The \CFE requires storage that atomically commits messages, identities and positions before
\opAck and supports consistent backup/restore. The durable registry retains owners,
source selections, acknowledged delivery positions and start/removal records;
subscribers retain atomic checkpoints. Store--\DC legs use TLS 1.3 with mutual
certificate authentication: each peer validates the other's certificate and binds
its authenticated identity to source/subscription permissions. The environment
supplies trusted certificates, credential provisioning and revocation; external
\AnalyticsClient connections use the same protection with read-only data scope.
We restrict database access; read access and wildcards grant no extra publishing scope.
UTC timestamps and monotonic timeout clocks keep recovery independent of clock adjustments.

\subparagraph{Rationale and examples.}
We choose asynchronous queues for unreliable distributed links
(Bass, Clements, and Kazman 224). Buffering keeps updates independent of the WAN;
acknowledging persistence keeps slow subscribers from delaying publishers. We use IDs
for safe retries and local filtering to decouple clients. We accept eventual consistency and
durable duplicate tracking, and must validate deployment limits and agree on wire binding.

\emph{Normal:} After a sale, the \StoreAgent atomically updates stock and
appends \texttt{e41} (\texttt{store/17}, \texttt{milk-1}, delta $-1$,
\texttt{2026-10-15T18:00:00.000Z}, sequence 41). The \Outbox sends
\opPublish; the \CFE persists, sends \opAck for \texttt{e41} through 41,
then fans out to subscriptions for that source. The \Outbox saves 41 before pruning;
subscribers apply once despite duplicates and send \opAck with \texttt{kind=applied}
and \texttt{appliedThrough=41} after their local commit.

\emph{Outage:} We follow the same source through WAN failure. The \Outbox buffers \texttt{e42,e43} while local updates continue.
On authenticated reconnect, it sends \opReplay: publisher role, source
\texttt{store/17}, saved cursor 41, events \texttt{[e42,e43]}, no subscription ID.
The \CFE persists, acknowledges and fans out each event; the \Outbox saves progress
before pruning. \Subscriber recovery first obtains \opResync watermarks, then
replays from its own durable cursor with its subscription ID and no event batch.
Identical retries are deduplicated; the \CFE retains records until subscriber
acknowledgements and the additional 60-minute window permit pruning.
