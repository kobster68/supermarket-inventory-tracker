\paragraph{\IEP interface specification.}
\label{sec:iep}
\subparagraph{Identity, purpose, and provided/required sides.}
Version 1.0 of the \IEP connects store/\DC publishers to runtime subscribers
through the \CFE.
The provided side comprises the six operations below. The required side comprises
durable storage, a subscriber registry, secure channels, a product catalog and clocks;
publishers retain unacknowledged events and subscribers process them idempotently
(Bass, Clements, and Kazman 230).

\subparagraph{Data types and topic naming.}
Case-sensitive fields and nonempty identifiers use exact comparison; wire encoding preserves types.

\begin{description}[nosep,leftmargin=1em,style=nextline]
\item[\InventoryEvent]
\texttt{eventId}: globally unique identifier, unchanged on retry;
\texttt{source}: \texttt{store/<storeId>} or \texttt{dc/<dcId>};
\texttt{product}: stable product identifier;
\texttt{quantityDelta}: nonzero signed 64-bit stock-unit delta (positive adds);
\texttt{timestamp}: UTC \texttt{YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss.sssZ};
\texttt{sequence}: persistent positive 64-bit counter, consecutive per source from 1.
Retries preserve all fields; sequence, not timestamp, determines order.

\item[\Acknowledgement]
\texttt{eventId}, \texttt{source}, \texttt{sequence}: accepted event;
\texttt{acceptedThrough}: highest contiguous persisted source sequence, not subscriber progress.

\item[Subscription]
\texttt{subscriberId}: stable authenticated client ID;
\texttt{subscriptionId}: identifier assigned by the \CFE;
\texttt{sourceFilter}: an exact source or \texttt{*}; and
\texttt{familyFilter}: an exact product-family path, that path followed by
\texttt{/**} for itself and all descendants, or \texttt{*} for all families.
Recovery positions are per subscription and source.
\end{description}

Topics: \texttt{inventory/<source>/family/<familyPath>}. Slash-separated segments
contain letters, digits, hyphens or underscores. The \CFE resolves catalog families;
both filters and authorization must match. \texttt{food/**} includes
\texttt{food/dairy}, not \texttt{foodstuff}.

\subparagraph{Resources and operations.}
Contracts specify syntax, effects and errors (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 219).
All requests require authenticated, authorized version-1.0 sessions; \texttt{?} means optional.
Common errors: \texttt{INVALID} (fields/version), \texttt{UNAUTHORIZED} (identity/scope),
\texttt{UNAVAILABLE}, \texttt{BUSY(retryAfterMs)} (positive millisecond delay),
\texttt{TIMEOUT} (uncertain outcome).

\begin{description}[nosep,leftmargin=1em,style=nextline]
\item[\opPublish\texttt{(event)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Submit an authorized source's next event.
\emph{Post:} Persist event and identity/sequence, then \opAck and fan-out;
identical retries create no extra entry.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{OUT\_OF\_ORDER} (returns expected sequence) for gaps,
\texttt{CONFLICT} for reused IDs/sequences with changed content,
\texttt{UNKNOWN\_PRODUCT}. Rejection never advances the source position.
\item[\opAck\texttt{(acknowledgement)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Broker response confirming durable acceptance.
\emph{Post:} The \Outbox saves the maximum of its position and
\texttt{acceptedThrough} before pruning; subscriber cursors are unchanged.
\emph{Errors:} Reject malformed/mismatched or unauthenticated confirmations as
\texttt{INVALID} or \texttt{UNAUTHORIZED} without pruning; lost responses cause retries.
\item[\opSubscribe\texttt{(subscriberId, sourceFilter, familyFilter)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Provisioned client opens a receive channel with authorized filters.
\emph{Post:} Persist/return subscription and \texttt{startAfter}, the per-source
accepted positions (zero if empty). Live events follow these positions; history
requires \opReplay. Identical active requests return the same record/positions.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIBER}; rejection creates nothing.
\item[\opUnsubscribe\texttt{(subscriberId, subscriptionId)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Owner removes its subscription.
\emph{Post:} Persist removal; stop scheduling deliveries, allowing in-flight messages.
Owner retries succeed while removal records exist; logs remain unchanged.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION} without active/removal record.
\item[\opReplay\texttt{(role, source, afterSequence, subscriptionId?, events?)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Nonnegative exclusive cursor. For \texttt{publisher}, send
ordered retained events after the \Outbox position; for \texttt{subscriber},
identify an owned active subscription, omit events and use its durable cursor.
\emph{Post:} Publisher events follow \opPublish individually; partial failure
identifies the first failed event. \Subscriber replay returns matching events in
source order through a captured high-water mark, then \texttt{scannedThrough}
(that mark); live delivery follows without gaps. Advance past filtered-out positions
only after processing returned events. Retries may duplicate delivery.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors and those of \opPublish; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION},
\texttt{INVALID\_CURSOR} (subscriber ahead), \texttt{HISTORY\_EXPIRED}.
Publisher-ahead positions require \opResync rather than skipping.
\item[\opResync\texttt{(role, source, lastKnownSequence, subscriptionId?)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Authorized publisher/subscriber reconciles a nonnegative
position; subscribers identify an owned active subscription.
\emph{Post:} Return \texttt{acceptedThrough}, \texttt{earliestRetained} (first
replayable sequence; accepted position plus one for an empty log), and recovery action.
Publishers resend after broker position; subscribers replay after their cursor.
No stock/sequence resets.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION},
\texttt{INVALID\_CURSOR} (subscriber ahead), \texttt{RECOVERY\_REQUIRED} with
bounds for missing history. Restore a consistent snapshot/backup and positions
before replay; reconciliation cannot reconstruct missing events.
\end{description}

\subparagraph{Shared semantics and restrictions.}
Restore writable logs, identity records, source positions and registry before serving.
One logical publisher owns each source: its \StoreAgent through the \Outbox, or
\DCAgent; failover prevents concurrent sequence allocation. Authorize every request/delivery.
Local inventory, sequence and \Outbox append commit atomically. Order per source,
not globally; subscribers atomically save changes, deduplication and processing cursors.
Retain identities across restarts throughout replay/retry history; unverifiable old events require recovery.

The \Outbox alone owns and saves publisher acknowledgement positions before pruning.
Subscribe before receiving; reconnect/replay from separate subscriber cursors.
Retry transient errors unchanged with capped exponential backoff/jitter, respecting
\texttt{retryAfterMs}; never discard unacknowledged events. Correct invalid requests;
use \opReplay or \opResync for gaps (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 228).
Bound batches/queues; return \texttt{BUSY} before admission. Isolate slow subscribers
for log-based catch-up. Offline updates continue until \Outbox exhaustion, which
rejects updates atomically. Expired history never silently advances cursors.

\subparagraph{Variability, limits, and quality properties.}
Required: 100 events/s/store, up to ten stores (1{,}000/s); provision additional
\DC, retry and fan-out capacity. Runtime \opSubscribe and \opUnsubscribe need
no restart/publisher change; declare tested client/queue limits and reject excess with
\texttt{BUSY}. Proposed, unvalidated: 2{,}000 store events/s during catch-up,
1--100-event replay batches, at least 60 minutes of replay/identity history,
extended for outage/catch-up headroom. Proposed 95th-percentile latency: 1 second
to \opAck, 2 seconds to first delivery, measured from transmission at live load
with healthy infrastructure, no backlog and within client limits; exclude processing.
Delivery is eventual, at least once, assuming restored connectivity, retry, sufficient
capacity and recoverable storage. Beyond retention, restore consistent snapshots/backups;
permanent loss of all copies is unrecoverable. System sizing: Section~\ref{sec:analysis-results}.

\subparagraph{Element requirements.}
Storage atomically commits events, identities and positions before \opAck, with
consistent backup/restore. The durable registry retains owners, filters and start/removal
records; subscribers retain atomic checkpoints. Supply catalog routing, encrypted
integrity-protected channels with mutual authentication (including local clients),
credentials, revocation and source/topic permissions. Restrict database access;
read access/wildcards grant no additional publishing scope. UTC timestamps and
monotonic timeout clocks keep recovery independent of clock adjustments.

\subparagraph{Rationale and examples.}
Asynchronous queues suit unreliable distributed links (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 224).
Buffering avoids WAN-dependent updates; persistence-boundary acknowledgement isolates
slow subscribers. IDs enable safe retries; topics decouple clients. Costs: eventual
consistency, durable duplicate tracking and deployment validation of limits/wire binding.

\emph{Normal:} After a POS sale, the \StoreAgent atomically updates stock and
appends \texttt{e41} (\texttt{store/17}, \texttt{milk-1}, delta $-1$,
\texttt{2026-10-15T18:00:00.000Z}, sequence 41). The \Outbox sends
\opPublish; the \CFE persists, sends \opAck for \texttt{e41} through 41,
then fans out to matching subscriptions. The \Outbox saves 41 before pruning;
subscribers apply once despite duplicates.

\emph{Outage:} The \Outbox buffers \texttt{e42,e43} while local updates continue.
On authenticated reconnect, it sends \opReplay: publisher role, source
\texttt{store/17}, saved cursor 41, events \texttt{[e42,e43]}, no subscription ID.
The \CFE persists, acknowledges and fans out each event; the \Outbox saves progress
before pruning. \Subscriber recovery uses its own cursor/subscription ID without an
event batch; \opResync reconciles missing history. Identical retries are deduplicated.
