\paragraph{\IEP interface specification.}
\label{sec:iep}
\subparagraph{Identity, purpose, and provided/required sides.}
Version 1.0 of the \IEP connects store/\DC publishers to runtime subscribers
through the \CFE.
The provided side comprises the six operations below. The required side comprises
durable storage, a subscriber registry, secure channels, a product catalog and clocks;
publishers retain unacknowledged events and subscribers process them idempotently.
We distinguish provided services from environmental needs (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 230).

\subparagraph{Data types and topic naming.}
Fields are case-sensitive; identifiers are nonempty strings compared exactly.
Wire encoding must preserve the following logical records.

\begin{description}[nosep,leftmargin=1em,style=nextline]
\item[\InventoryEvent]
\texttt{eventId}: globally unique identifier, unchanged on retry;
\texttt{source}: \texttt{store/<storeId>} or \texttt{dc/<dcId>};
\texttt{product}: stable product identifier;
\texttt{quantityDelta}: signed 64-bit integer in the product's catalogued stock
unit (positive adds stock, negative removes it; zero is invalid);
\texttt{timestamp}: UTC occurrence time in
\texttt{YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss.sssZ} form; and
\texttt{sequence}: positive 64-bit integer, consecutive per source from 1, persistent across
restarts/reconnects; timestamps do not determine order.
Retries preserve all fields; an ID always identifies the same record.

\item[\Acknowledgement]
\texttt{eventId}, \texttt{source} and \texttt{sequence} identify the accepted event;
\texttt{acceptedThrough} is the highest contiguous source sequence persisted by the \CFE;
confirmation excludes subscriber processing. Failures return errors.

\item[Subscription]
\texttt{subscriberId}: stable authenticated client ID;
\texttt{subscriptionId}: identifier assigned by the \CFE;
\texttt{sourceFilter}: an exact source or \texttt{*}; and
\texttt{familyFilter}: an exact product-family path, that path followed by
\texttt{/**} for itself and all descendants, or \texttt{*} for all families.
Recovery positions are per subscription and source.
\end{description}

Topics are \texttt{inventory/<source>/family/<familyPath>}, for example
\texttt{inventory/store/17/family/food/dairy}. ID/path segments contain letters,
digits, hyphens or underscores; slashes separate levels. The \CFE resolves product
families from the catalog. Both filters and topic authorization must match;
\texttt{food/**} includes \texttt{food/dairy}, not \texttt{foodstuff}.

\subparagraph{Resources and operations.}
We specify operation syntax, effects and errors (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 219).
Logical signatures below require an authenticated, authorized version-1.0 session;
\texttt{?} marks optional arguments.
Common errors are \texttt{INVALID} (malformed fields), \texttt{UNAUTHORIZED}
(identity or scope denied), \texttt{UNAVAILABLE} (required service unavailable),
\texttt{BUSY(retryAfterMs)} (capacity exhausted), and \texttt{TIMEOUT}.
\texttt{retryAfterMs} is a positive integer delay in milliseconds.
Timeouts leave outcomes uncertain; retry unchanged.

\begin{description}[nosep,leftmargin=1em,style=nextline]
\item[\opPublish\texttt{(event)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Submit an authorized source's next event.
\emph{Post:} Persist event and identity/sequence, then \opAck and fan-out;
identical retries create no extra entry.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{OUT\_OF\_ORDER(expectedSequence)} for gaps,
\texttt{CONFLICT} for reused IDs/sequences with changed content,
\texttt{UNKNOWN\_PRODUCT}. Rejection never advances the source position.
\item[\opAck\texttt{(acknowledgement)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Broker response confirming durable acceptance.
\emph{Post:} The \Outbox saves the maximum of its position and
\texttt{acceptedThrough} before pruning; subscriber cursors are unchanged.
\emph{Errors:} Reject malformed/mismatched or unauthenticated confirmations as
\texttt{INVALID} or \texttt{UNAUTHORIZED} without pruning; lost responses cause retries.
\item[\opSubscribe\texttt{(subscriberId, sourceFilter, familyFilter)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Provisioned client opens a receive channel with authorized filters.
\emph{Post:} Persist/return subscription and \texttt{startAfter}, the per-source
accepted positions (zero if empty). Live events follow these positions; history
requires \opReplay. Identical active requests return the same record/positions.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIBER}; rejection creates nothing.
\item[\opUnsubscribe\texttt{(subscriberId, subscriptionId)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Owner removes its subscription.
\emph{Post:} Persist removal; stop scheduling deliveries, allowing in-flight messages.
Owner retries succeed while removal records exist; logs remain unchanged.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION} without active/removal record.
\item[\opReplay\texttt{(role, source, afterSequence, subscriptionId?, events?)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Nonnegative exclusive cursor. For \texttt{publisher}, send
ordered retained events after the \Outbox position; for \texttt{subscriber},
identify an owned active subscription, omit events and use its durable cursor.
\emph{Post:} Publisher events follow \opPublish individually; partial failure
identifies the first failed event. Subscriber replay returns matching events in
source order through a captured high-water mark, then \texttt{scannedThrough}
(that mark); live delivery follows without gaps. Advance past filtered-out positions
only after processing returned events. Retries may duplicate delivery.
\emph{Errors:} Common/publish errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION},
\texttt{INVALID\_CURSOR} (subscriber ahead), \texttt{HISTORY\_EXPIRED}.
Publisher-ahead positions require \opResync rather than skipping.
\item[\opResync\texttt{(role, source, lastKnownSequence, subscriptionId?)}]
\emph{Pre/meaning:} Authorized publisher/subscriber reconciles a nonnegative
position; subscribers identify an owned active subscription.
\emph{Post:} Return \texttt{acceptedThrough}, \texttt{earliestRetained} (first
replayable sequence; accepted position plus one for an empty log), and recovery action.
Publishers resend after broker position; subscribers replay after their cursor.
No stock/sequence resets.
\emph{Errors:} Common errors; \texttt{UNKNOWN\_SUBSCRIPTION},
\texttt{INVALID\_CURSOR} (subscriber ahead), \texttt{RECOVERY\_REQUIRED} with
bounds for missing history. Restore a consistent snapshot/backup and positions
before replay; reconciliation cannot reconstruct missing events.
\end{description}

\subparagraph{Shared semantics, errors, and usage restrictions.}
Restore writable logs, identity records, source positions and registry before serving.
Permit one logical publisher per source: its \StoreAgent through the \Outbox,
or the \DCAgent; failover prevents concurrent sequence allocation.
Authorize every request/delivery; read permission never grants publish permission.
Local inventory, sequence allocation and \Outbox append commit atomically.
Order events per source, not globally; subscribers atomically save applied changes,
event-ID deduplication and processing cursors. Identity records survive restarts
throughout the retry/replay window; unverifiable old events require recovery.

The \Outbox alone owns the last acknowledged publisher sequence and saves it before
pruning. Subscribe before receiving; reconnect with the existing subscription and
replay from each subscriber cursor, not the publisher position. Retry transient
errors unchanged with capped exponential backoff and jitter, waiting at least
\texttt{retryAfterMs} for \texttt{BUSY}. Never discard unacknowledged events after
a retry limit. Correct invalid/unauthorized/conflicting requests; replay/resync gaps.
Retrying transient failures and correcting invalid requests follow the book's
error-handling guidance (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 228).

Bound batches and per-client queues; return \texttt{BUSY} before accepting new
work. Pause/disconnect slow subscribers independently for log-based catch-up.
Local updates continue offline while \Outbox capacity remains; exhaustion rejects
updates atomically and reports a fault. Expired history never silently advances cursors.

\subparagraph{Variability, limits, and quality properties.}
Required workload: 100 events/s/store, up to ten stores (1{,}000/s), plus capacity
for \DC traffic, retries and fan-out. Runtime \opSubscribe and \opUnsubscribe requires
no publisher changes or broker restart. Deployments declare tested subscription/queue
limits; exceeding capacity returns \texttt{BUSY}. Proposed, unvalidated settings:
2{,}000 store events/s during catch-up, replay batches of 1--100, and at least
60 minutes of replay/identity history, extended for outage/catch-up headroom.
Proposed latency: 95th percentile within 1 second to \opAck and 2 seconds to
first delivery, measured from transmission at live load, healthy infrastructure,
within client limits and without backlog; application processing is excluded.
System sizing/availability analysis is in Section~\ref{sec:analysis-results}.

Delivery is eventual and at least once, conditional on eventual connectivity,
continued retry, adequate capacity and recoverable durable storage. Subscriber
catch-up is bounded by retention; older gaps require consistent snapshot/backup recovery.
Idempotency prevents double application; permanent loss of all copies is not recoverable.

\subparagraph{Element requirements.}
Storage atomically commits events, identities and positions before \opAck,
with consistent backup/restore. The durable registry retains ownership, filters,
start/removal records; subscribers retain atomic checkpoints. The environment supplies product-family routing data,
encrypted integrity-protected channels with client/server authentication, credentials,
revocation and source/topic authorization, including local clients. Restrict direct
database access; wildcards grant no extra permission. UTC clocks timestamp events;
monotonic clocks measure timeouts. Sequence-based recovery tolerates clock adjustment.

\subparagraph{Rationale and design issues.}
We use asynchronous queued events for unreliable distributed communication
(Bass, Clements, and Kazman 224). Buffering avoids WAN-dependent updates; persistence-boundary acknowledgement
isolates slow subscribers. IDs make retries safe, source ordering avoids global
coordination, and topics decouple clients. Costs: eventual consistency and durable
duplicate tracking. Deployment must validate limits and agree on wire encoding.

\subparagraph{Example exchanges.}
\emph{Normal:} With positions at 40 and an authorized dairy subscription active,
a POS sale reaches the \POSAdapter and \StoreAgent. One transaction subtracts stock
and appends \texttt{e41} (\texttt{store/17}, \texttt{milk-1}, delta $-1$,
\texttt{2026-10-15T18:00:00.000Z}, sequence 41). The \Outbox sends
\opPublish\texttt{(e41)}; the \CFE persists, returns \opAck identifying
\texttt{e41} with \texttt{acceptedThrough=41}, then fans out. The \Outbox saves
41 before pruning; subscribers apply once even if retries duplicate delivery.

\emph{Outage:} With the \Outbox position at 41, local transactions buffer
\texttt{e42,e43} during WAN failure. After reconnecting and authenticating, it sends
\opReplay with \texttt{role=publisher}, \texttt{source=store/17},
\texttt{afterSequence=41}, no subscription ID and \texttt{events=[e42,e43]}.
Each event is persisted, acknowledged and fanned out; the \Outbox saves each
position before pruning. Accepted retries are deduplicated. A disconnected
subscriber instead replays from its own cursor with its subscription ID and no
event batch; \opResync handles position/history discrepancies.
